# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users
The public: anyone who finds the project online, often on a phone, who is curious about what actually happens to a body in a common accident. They watch, scrub and read; they are not clinicians and are not in an emergency.

## Product Purpose
Accident Lab teaches five accidents (pedestrian hit by a car, rip-current drowning, fall from height, electrocution, house-fire burns) three ways at once: the physics and engineering numbers, a 3D simulation driven by those numbers, and the medical view (mechanism, prevention, first aid, hospital care, surgery). Success is a visitor who leaves understanding why speed, height, current or heat does the damage, and what to do and not do.

## Positioning
Every number on screen is computed by `lab/physics.py` and the same keyframes drive both the Blender video and the live Three.js scene, so the explanation, the animation and the graphs cannot disagree.

## Operating Context
Static site served from `web/` (python http.server locally). Data comes from `web/data.js` (`window.SIM_DATA`) generated by `lab/build.py`. Rendered vertical videos (720×1280) live in `out/<id>.mp4`, rendered by `blender/render_scenario.py`.

## Capabilities and Constraints
- Video and live 3D play side by side on one shared timeline; the visitor can orbit the 3D view while the video shows the directed shot.
- Plain static HTML/JS, Three.js 0.160 from a CDN, no build step.
- Surgery content is descriptive and educational only, never instructions.

## Brand Commitments
The rendered videos set the visual world (user-supplied reference video): white clay technical illustration, ink outlines, one crimson accent, serif bold titles with italic subtitles, leader-line callouts, graphs with equations.

## Evidence on Hand
All figures come from `lab/physics.py`; medical text from `lab/medical.py`. No testimonials, users or endorsements exist; none may be invented.

## Product Principles
1. The numbers lead: every claim shows its value and how it was computed.
2. One timeline: video, 3D, HUD and graphs always show the same instant.
3. Calm, not gory: illustrate injury mechanically, never sensationally.
4. Safety first: emergency numbers and first aid stay one tap away.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Phone-first reading, readable without sound; respect reduced-motion for autoplay.
